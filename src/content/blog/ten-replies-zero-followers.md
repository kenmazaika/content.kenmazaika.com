**TL;DR**

- **What it's about:** my reply-growth engine on X is failing — ten replies, zero follows, goal of five a day — while every reply-guy case study that publishes real numbers ran 100–250 replies a day. Ehsan's 20–50 a day made ~3.5 follows; 100–150 a day made 15–20. CRANQ logged one follow per ten replies at ~250 a day.
- **What to do differently:** set the volume dial from the goal before touching reply quality — five follows a day is roughly 35–50 replies a day in 3–4 paced sessions, with profile visits as the daily KPI and a 60-day judgment window. Never burst: 20+ replies an hour, copy-paste, pestering one account, or links in the reply trip X's spam filters for 24–72 hours.
- **The key takeaway:** a reply is an impression, not a follow — its ceiling is the audience of the post you reply to at that moment — so reply growth is a throughput-and-placement pipeline, and "just write better replies" is what the tool sellers say.

This afternoon I opened my reply-growth list: seventy accounts in the AI-agent niche, curated over weeks. Ten replies in, each specific, each carrying real experience: zero follows. So I screenshotted a post with 92 views and two replies and asked my agent which reply would get me a follow. Wrong question. The reply isn't the product — the people who've actually grown this way treat replies as inventory, and the gap between their numbers and mine isn't quality. It's throughput, about ten times over.

Read the logs, not the advice. The cleanest log is Ehsan Yazdanparast's ([@acadictive, April 2026](https://ehsan-yazdanparast.medium.com/how-the-reply-guy-strategy-on-x-helped-me-gain-1-000-followers-in-2-months-402657124699)). Phase one: 57 days of 3–4 posts a day plus 20–50 replies inside build-in-public circles → 200 followers. About 3.5 follows a day, at two to five times the volume I was running. Phase two: he went reply-first, 100–150 replies a day → 15–20 follows a day, 30–50 on the good days. Same account, same niche. His replies didn't get better; the volume went up three to five times and compounding started. Cost: about three hours a day, split into 3–4 sessions.

CRANQ ([@CRANQNow, September 2024](https://medium.com/@tobyro/hey-i-know-you-but-you-dont-know-me-i-m-reply-guy-136792f31b02)) ran the corporate version: roughly 60 followers in March, ~1,200 by September, aiming from May at ~250 replies a day. Their tracked month averaged one follow per ten replies, and their note on why it works is the whole mechanism: "as a small account, most of your profile visits will come from being seen in other people's posts."

TomHacks ([November 2025](https://tomhacks.com/post/reply-guy-part-1)) ran the purest version — he wanted impressions, not followers. 440 replies in a day → 905k impressions. 611 replies → 1.36M. A peak day at 2.46M. The data point that matters: replies keep earning for about 24 hours after you post them. A reply is a small advertisement running on someone else's traffic.

Now compare that with the advice I was handed this morning: 10–20 thoughtful replies a day, first 1,000 followers in 2–4 months. It comes from [VoicePost](https://voicepo.st/blog/reply-guy-method-first-1000-followers), a reply-tool company whose own product caps at fifteen drafts a day. Nice advice. No logs. The people above published their numbers, and the smallest daily dial among them — 20–50 — is the phase Ehsan calls the warm-up.

Why the dial beats the words: a reply is a candidate, not a broadcast. X decides whether it surfaces beyond the thread it lands in, and the thread's audience is the ceiling. Ten replies to posts pulling a few hundred views is a few hundred impressions total. At the logged conversion rates — CRANQ's one follow per ten replies, Ehsan's organic one in 20–30 — a few hundred impressions is a rounding error. Volume is how you buy enough impressions for the ratio to matter. Placement keeps the cost low: fresh posts, the first hour, active authors, two or three communities, accounts with balanced follow ratios.

Follows lag the work. Ehsan's phase one — the "only" 3.5 follows a day — built the familiarity and the author reply-loops that made phase two convert. Judge the experiment at day 60, not day 3. My ten replies weren't a failed experiment; they were a sample size of ten.

The catch: the dial has a ceiling, and it's pacing, not willpower. X's filters trip on patterns — the 2026 [reply-reach audits](https://reachmore.co/blogs/why-x-replies-get-no-views) are specific: 20+ an hour reads as automated, copy-pasted replies get flagged, three or four to one account a day looks like pestering, links in the reply pull people off-platform — and recovery runs 24–72 hours. Ehsan's rules for surviving 100+ a day: split into 3–4 sessions, never templates, never AI-generated replies ("people notice. Algorithms notice."), no "let's connect" cold-opens. TomHacks, pushing 600 a day, wasn't banned — he was blocked by actual humans. That's the "I want to be human" constraint made concrete: the volume is only sustainable if it's genuinely human, varied, and paced. It's also why the list of seventy feels broken: the unit isn't accounts, it's fresh posts — fifty replies a day means finding fifty reply-worthy posts within their first hour, every day. That's a surfacing pipeline (the agent finds and drafts, the human owns voice and send), not a list.

So the math for the goal. Five follows a day is roughly 35–50 replies a day at the logged ratios, sustained, with the first months underperforming while the loop warms. If three hours a day of replying isn't a life you want — it is a job, Ehsan is candid about that — the honest dial is 20–30 replies a day for one to three follows; set the goal to the dial you'll actually turn. What exists in no log is ten replies a day returning five follows. Set the dial, watch profile visits, and only then decide whether the goal was wrong.
