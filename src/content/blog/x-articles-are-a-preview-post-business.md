I upgraded to X Premium to publish Articles and found out the $3 tier doesn't include them. That's the first thing nobody tells you about X Articles. The second is bigger: the article body isn't the distribution unit — the preview post is. If the cover, headline, and excerpt don't hook, nothing else matters. I ran the research, tested the posting routes, and this is the playbook I'm building the factory from: flagship post in, paste-ready Article out, agent doing the rewrite and you doing the launch.

## The tier gate comes first

Articles opened to all Premium subscribers in January 2026; before that it was Premium+ only. The official help page is explicit: publishing Articles is limited to Premium and up — free accounts and Basic subscribers can't publish them. Basic is the $3 tier. It gets you 25,000-character posts, a one-hour edit window, and a small reply boost. Articles need the $8 Premium tier. Premium+ is $40, ad-free, with the highest reply prioritization.

Check your actual tier in-app before building anything. Tiers vary by region and the marketing pages blur them, so the first stage of my pipeline verifies the tier and stops if it's wrong. Everything downstream is wasted work if the account can't publish.

## The preview post is the product

Articles don't get distributed like tweets. They run through a preview post in the feed — cover, headline, excerpt — and that preview is the hook. Treat it like a tweet: specific, one strong claim, something worth a click. The body does different work. Long dwell time — five-to-ten-minute reads — and bookmarks are the signals X is rewarding on Articles, and question-format headings get your Article cited in Grok answers over time. That's a compounding discovery surface threads don't have.

There's a third mechanic worth knowing: Articles are the one X format where your blog link doesn't cost you reach. External links in regular posts get punished hard — practitioner analyses put the visibility cut in the 30–94% range. Inside an Article, links are fine. X keeps people on-platform and rewards that.

## The rewrite is a rewrite

An Article is not your blog post pasted in. The working range for engagement is 500–1,500 words, with strong performers running 800–2,000, so the flagship gets compressed, not copied. Structure for mobile skimming: short paragraphs, an H2 every few paragraphs, bullets where the original had paragraphs. Lead with the definition of the claim so Grok can extract it. Close with numbered takeaways and a real question — questions are what start reply chains.

The agent does the compression, and the cross-check is the same one every factory stage has: every claim in the Article still traces to a receipt in the original post. No new numbers, no invented quotes.

## The cover is a spec, not a design task

X doesn't publish official dimensions. The practitioner-tested standard is 1920×368 — a 5.2:1 ultrawide — with text strictly centered because the edges clip, under 5MB, sRGB. Sixteen-by-nine gets cropped and underperforms. Set a branded template once and every Article reuses it: same palette, same layout, so the preview reads as yours before anyone reads a word.

## Posting routes, priced

Your agent has three ways to post, and they cost very different things.

The API is pay-per-use now — the legacy $200/month Basic tier was deprecated in June. Current rates: $0.015 per plain post, $0.20 per post containing a URL. At one or two Articles plus a teaser thread a week, that's a couple of dollars a month.

Premium+ is the sleeper: as of late August it includes $20/month in API credits. For a normal blog's distribution volume the API is effectively free — and you get the Articles access and reply prioritization on the same subscription.

Browser automation is the free route, and I tested it today: pulled my X session cookies from a Firefox profile into an isolated Chromium on a virtual display, composed a real draft, confirmed the Post button was live, and discarded it. Nothing posted. It works, it costs nothing, it's against X's terms of service, and it breaks whenever the UI changes or the session invalidates. Fine for an experiment. Not for something you'd be sad to lose the account over.

Scrappy but not manual: the agent drafts everything and hands you one paste-ready file. Publishing is a single action — yours, not the agent's.

## The factory

Four stages, same skeleton as the Reddit retrofit factory I run in Claw: research (verify the tier, pick the flagship, define the angle), rewrite (compress with the structure rules above), assets (cover at spec, plus a three-to-five-post teaser thread), and a verify gate that blocks anything failing the checklist — tier verified, 800–1,500 words, question-format H2s, cover at spec, no pasted walls of blog text. Output is one file: title, Article body, cover spec, teaser thread, launch timing, first-hour reply plan.

## Launch and measure

Publish the Article standalone, then burst the teaser thread immediately — threads win the first velocity, Articles win the long tail. Pin the Article for 24–72 hours. Then be present for the first hour: an author reply back is weighted roughly 150× a like in the open-sourced ranking, and ten replies in the first fifteen minutes triggers the out-of-network cascade. The first-hour reply count is your launch metric, not first-hour views.

Verdict at 48 hours, not two: dwell, bookmarks, profile visits, replies. Articles live for days, and the Grok citations compound from there.

Check the tier first. Then let the agent do the rewrite and you do the launch. That's the whole game: the preview post is the product, and it's the only X format where your blog link is free.
