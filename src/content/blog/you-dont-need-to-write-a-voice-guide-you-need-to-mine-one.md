You've already written your voice guide. It's sitting in your Hermes message history — every correction you gave a draft, every dictation, every "voice is flat" and "cut the filler." You were editing a style guide in real time. You just weren't keeping it.

Stop writing the guide. Mine it.

*(This nudges my last post. I said the [guide only works if you write it yourself](/blog/you-dont-have-a-writing-problem-you-have-an-approval-problem/) — still true. The honest follow-up is that you've been writing it all along, one correction at a time. A blank page just produces a wish list, not a voice.)*

## The method

Three stages, all in Hermes.

**Export.** Pull your genuine messages out of Hermes's session database. Genuine means the messages you actually typed or dictated — not the system summaries and background-job noise in the same database. Filter, deduplicate, and you've got a clean corpus of your actual voice.

**Mine.** The corpus is too big to read whole. Split it by time, run parallel passes across the slices, and pull out verbatim quotes with timestamps: your voice, your positions, your corrections. The exact words. Every line in the guide then traces to something you actually said.

**Build.** Merge it into a guidebook: what to emulate, what to strip, beliefs, recurring themes.

The catch: your raw voice includes the hesitations — "I feel like," "kind of," "sort of." Natural dictating into a phone, death in a published piece. So the rule that makes the whole thing usable: **the guide tells the AI to mimic your certainty, your cadence, and your judgment — not your hesitations.**

## The prompt

Here's the whole workflow as a prompt you can run in Hermes:

```text
Build a content voice guide and beliefs file for me by mining my own message history with my AI assistant. Do it in three stages: export, mine, build.

Stage 1 — Export. My messages live in Hermes profile SQLite session databases at ~/.hermes/profiles/<profile>/state.db. A profile is a folder under ~/.hermes/profiles/. Pull out only my genuine messages — the ones I typed or dictated — by taking role='user' rows where the session's source is one of telegram, cli, or email. Exclude auto-generated system rows: anything starting with [CONTEXT COMPACTION, [ASYNC DELEGATION BATCH COMPLETE, [memory-context], <memory-context>, or [SYSTEM. Deduplicate (the same message often recurs under multiple timestamps). Write the cleaned corpus to one text file, one message per block with a ### [<UTC timestamp>] <profile>/<source> header.

Stage 2 — Mine. The corpus is too big to read whole, so split it into ~6 chronological slices of equal message count and dispatch a parallel analysis agent per slice. Each agent must read its ENTIRE slice and extract, with verbatim quotes (keep exact wording + timestamp):
- Natural prose/dictation samples revealing voice (phrasing, rhythm, rhetorical moves, catchphrases, how ideas get framed)
- Stated opinions, positions, and beliefs — things I assert, defend, or push back on
- Corrections or feedback I give on AI-generated writing (what I like/dislike about tone, voice, style, structure)
- Explicit preference statements about voice/tone/style
- Recurring themes/obsessions and my attitude toward them
Skip operational task requests ("find X", "do Y"). Instruct agents never to fabricate quotes — only quote text actually in the file. Collect all six reports.

Stage 3 — Build. Synthesize the reports into a markdown guidebook with these sections:
1. Voice — what to emulate: signature moves (lead with the punchline; matter-of-fact operator tone; conversational-not-academic; connected prose not staccato; concrete real numbers; decisive; clear), vocabulary/catchphrases, length & shape.
2. Do NOT emulate — strip when building content: hedging ("kind of," "sort of," "maybe"), "I feel like" used as a hedge (keep only the opinion-anchoring use), dictation filler ("It's like," "you know"), mid-thought run-ons, self-undercutting in authority pieces. Add a "words/moves never to include" list (no framework mnemonics, no hype/over-claims, no invented numbers, no marketing-y clickbait).
3. Beliefs — 10-15 things I'd defend in a room, each with a verbatim supporting quote.
4. Recurring themes for framing content.
Keep every claim grounded in a verbatim quote with its timestamp. The guidebook's purpose is content production: AI should mimic my certainty, cadence, and judgment — not my hesitations.
```

## Why it wins

Because it's yours, and you can't fake that. A mined guide is what you actually sound like, extracted from 2,242 of your own messages. When the verifier pass checks a draft against it, it's checking against the real thing.

You don't have a writing problem. And you don't have a voice-guide problem either. You have a mining problem — and mining is the one part a machine is actually good at.
